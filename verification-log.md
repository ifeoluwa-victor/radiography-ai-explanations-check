# Verification Log

Date checked: 19 October 2026
Sources were located with AI help (Claude). Each link should be opened and read before relying on it.

| # | AI-style claim | Source checked | Problem found | Corrected explanation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Ultrasound uses low-dose X-rays to create images of the baby." | [FDA: Ultrasound Imaging](https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/medical-imaging/ultrasound-imaging) | Hallucination. The FDA page says ultrasound uses high-frequency sound waves and involves no ionising radiation exposure. | Ultrasound uses high-frequency sound waves, not X-rays. |
| 2 | "A pregnancy scan always gives the exact due date, at any stage." | [ACOG Committee Opinion No. 700, Methods for Estimating the Due Date (2017), copy hosted by the University of Iowa](https://www.healthcare.uiowa.edu/familymedicine/fpinfo/OB/OB2017/ACOG%20redating%20gestational%20age.pdf) | Overconfident. The opinion says first-trimester ultrasound is the most accurate method, and that measurements are more accurate the earlier in the first trimester they are taken. It describes an estimated due date, not an exact one. | Scans estimate gestational age and due date. They are most reliable early in pregnancy and less precise later. |
| 3 | "MRI is safe for everyone because it uses no radiation." | [RadiologyInfo.org: MRI Safety](https://radiologyinfo.org/en/info/mr) | Missing context. The page confirms MRI uses no ionising radiation, but says the strong magnetic field can affect some implants and devices, so patients are screened first. | MRI uses no ionising radiation, but safety screening for implants and metal is essential. |
| 4 | "This AI tool detects abnormalities with 99% accuracy, so radiologists are no longer needed." | [Harvard Medical School: Does AI help or hurt human radiologists' performance?](https://hms.harvard.edu/news/does-ai-help-or-hurt-human-radiologists-performance-depends-doctor) | Unsupported conclusion. The 99% figure has no source. The study of 140 radiologists found AI helped some and worsened others, and that poorly performing AI tools reduced accuracy. | A figure needs a source, and results depend on the tool and the setting. AI output needs checking by clinicians. |
| 5 | "CT is always better than ultrasound." | [ACR Appropriateness Criteria: Right Upper Quadrant Pain](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4332791) and [AAFP: Diagnostic Imaging of Acute Abdominal Pain in Adults](https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2015/0401/p452.html) | Overconfident. For suspected acute cholecystitis with right upper quadrant pain, ultrasound is the usual first test. For right or left lower quadrant pain in adults, CT is the usual first test. | The best test depends on the clinical question and the patient. |

## Notes and limits
- The ACR criteria I found are an earlier version. ACR publishes updates, so the current version should be checked.
- The ACOG link is a copy of the opinion. ACOG's own website is the official source.
